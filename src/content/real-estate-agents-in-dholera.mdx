Almost every Dholera land purchase runs through an agent — and in a region where the same
village contains plots with different TP schemes, road widths and development rights, a good
agent is genuinely valuable. A bad one is the most expensive thing you will ever buy.

This is how to tell them apart. It comes down to four checks and five questions.

> **Start with the legal baseline.** In Gujarat, real-estate agents should operate under
> **Gujarat RERA** registration. Ask any agent for their **RERA registration number** and
> verify it on the Gujarat RERA portal. An agent who cannot produce one is operating outside
> the statutory framework designed to protect you — that alone should end the conversation.

## Check 1: RERA registration and identity

- Ask for the **RERA registration number** and check it is current.
- Confirm the **actual registered name** and that the person you are dealing with is
  authorised to act for that registration.
- Be cautious if the agent is also the **seller's representative** or a **relative of the
  owner** — that is a conflict of interest, and their duty is not to you. A genuine buyer's
  agent represents your side of the table only.

## Check 2: Do they actually know the parcel?

This is the test that separates professionals from lead-sellers. A knowledgeable Dholera agent
should be able to answer, for any parcel they offer:

- Which **TP scheme** the plot falls in, and whether that scheme is **serviced** yet.
- The **abutting road width** — because under **DGDCR** this sets the permissible
  **FAR (1.20–4.00)** and building height (**15–70 m**), i.e. what you may build.
- Whether reconstitution is **gazetted**, and the **Form 4/5 Final Plot** number and area.
- Whether the land use is **NA-converted** or still agricultural on the 7/12.
- How the asking price compares to the **DICDL rate card** and the village's indicative band.

If the answer to all of these is "the price is going up, decide quickly", you are not speaking
to an agent who has done the work. Cross-check anything you are told against the
[interactive cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) and the
[TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map) yourself.

## Check 3: Do they welcome independent verification?

A trustworthy agent **wants** you to verify. They will encourage you to:

- Pull the **7/12 directly from AnyRoR** yourself (follow the
  [7/12 verification workflow](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records)).
- Search the **survey number** on the [cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) and confirm
  the boundaries and FP number.
- Benchmark the price against the
  [village-wise land price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-village-wise-land-prices).

An agent who resists independent verification, rushes you, or insists on a "token" payment to
"hold" the plot before due diligence is telling you exactly who they are. **Walk away.**

## Check 4: Are they pricing honestly?

Dholera has **no official per-plot transaction price** — only the **DICDL allotment card**
(a 99-year leasehold priced by land-use zone, updated 24 August 2026), which is a floor, not a
market price. An honest agent will:

- Distinguish an **Original Plot (OP)** quote from a **Final Plot (FP)** quote, and explain
  that under land pooling roughly **50% of the OP area is contributed** to infrastructure —
  so an OP at ₹3,000/sq yd that yields a 50% FP is effectively **₹6,000/sq yd** of
  deliverable land.
- Separate **serviced** Activation Area pricing (up to ~₹21,000/sq yd industrial,
  ₹11,000–16,000 residential) from **unserviced** TP 3–6 pricing (₹8,000–12,000).
- Remind you that **stamp duty (4.9%) + registration (1%) + NA premium (10% of jantri)** sit
  on top of the price, and that **jantri doubled statewide in April 2023**.

An agent who quotes a single headline number and none of the above is quoting a number, not a
cost. Read the [DICDL rate card analysis](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## The five questions to ask verbatim

1. *"What is your Gujarat RERA registration number, and who in this transaction do you
   represent?"*
2. *"Which TP scheme is this plot in, and is trunk infrastructure live there yet?"*
3. *"What is the abutting road width, and what FAR and height does DGDCR allow me on it?"*
4. *"Is reconstitution gazetted? Show me the Form 4/5 Final Plot number and area."*
5. *"Is the land use NA-converted on the current 7/12? I will pull it myself from AnyRoR."*

Three or more vague answers means the agent has not done the work on the parcel they are
selling you.

## Red flags

- Urgency tactics: "only one plot left", "price rises tomorrow".
- Refusal to let you see the 7/12 or search the survey number yourself.
- A "token" payment demanded before due diligence.
- Quoting the OP price while showing FP build-out, or vice versa.
- The agent is related to, or employed by, the seller.
- Pricing that assumes servicing or road frontage the parcel does not have.
- No written record — everything only on calls and messages.
