Dholera SIR is not one market — it is twenty-two. Land in a serviced TP 1 village trades at
a completely different price to land in an unserviced TP 6 coastal village, even when both
are "in Dholera". This is the 2026 village-wise breakdown: every revenue village, its Town
Planning scheme, its functional zone, and an indicative asking-rate band.

> **Methodology, read first.** Dholera has **no official per-village price** — DICDL prices by
> land-use zone only, and no body publishes a verified transaction figure for a named parcel.
> What follows is our reading of the September 2026 broker inventory, mapped onto each
> village's gazetted TP scheme and functional zone. Treat these as **asking-rate context, not
> market data**. For the authoritative explanation of what a Dholera "official" price does and
> does not cover, read the [DICDL rate card analysis](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## The official floor: DICDL rates (unchanged at August 2026)

The one authoritative published figure is the **DICDL allotment card** of 24 August 2026 — a
**99-year leasehold** quoted per square metre, by land-use zone: roughly **₹8,000/sq m** for
city-centre and tourism uses, **₹6,000** for residential and high-access-corridor, and
**₹4,000** for industry, knowledge and recreation (solar park land is a nominal ₹400).

That card is a **floor, not a transaction price**. It omits GST, registration, stamp duty and
land-filling, and it quotes serviced and unserviced parcels identically — which is precisely
why the market trades above it and why villages differ so widely. The full rate-card breakdown
is in the [plot price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026);
the village spread below is what that card does not explain.

## What actually sets the village price

Three things, in order of importance:

1. **Servicing status.** A village with live trunk infrastructure (underground power, water,
   effluent, optical fibre) trades far above a gazetted-but-unserviced one. This is the
   single biggest driver — bigger than the zone label.
2. **TP scheme and road frontage.** Your permissible FAR (1.20–4.00) and building height
   (15–70 m) under DGDCR are set by the **abutting road width**, so a plot on a 55 m TP road
   is worth materially more than an identical plot on an 18 m road in the same village.
3. **Anchor proximity.** Villages adjoining the Tata semiconductor fab corridor and the
   expressway interchanges command a premium over peripheral coastal villages.

## The three tiers, explained

**Tier 1 — TP 1 serviced (₹11,000 – 16,000+/sq yd).** Ambli, Bhadana, Bhadiyad, Bhimtalav,
Gogla, Kadipur, Khun and Umargadh. These carry the Activation Area and live trunk
infrastructure. Plots abutting the **55 m TP roads** sit at the top of the band
(₹14,000 – 16,000+); interior parcels sit lower. This is where delivered infrastructure, not
the zone label, sets the price.

**Tier 2 — TP 2 / TP 3 / TP 4 corridor (₹11,000 – 15,000, industrial up to ~₹21,000).**
Bhimnath, Gorasu, Hebatpur, Otariya, Pipli, Rahtalav, Sandhida, Mundi. The spread here is
wide on purpose: **serviced industrial** land in the live-trunk corridor trades *above*
residential land (the inversion that surprises most first-time buyers), while unserviced
parcels in the same villages trade closer to the TP 3–5 residential band of
₹9,000 – 12,000. Hebatpur and Pipli carry premiums for fab-corridor and interchange
proximity respectively.

**Tier 3 — peripheral TP 5 / TP 6 (₹8,000 – 9,500).** Bavaliyari, Bhangadh, Zankhi, plus the
outer TP 3–4 villages. Gazetted and planned, but trunk infrastructure is still rolling out.
Prices here reflect **future potential more than present servicing** — the highest
speculation and the longest hold.

## Why the headline price is not what you pay

Every band above is quoted on **what you are being sold** — but in Dholera, what you are
sold and what you receive are different sizes. Land here is pooled, not acquired: your
Original Plot is reconstituted under the Gujarat town-planning and SIR statutes, with roughly
half its area contributed to roads, utilities and parks, and a smaller serviced Final Plot
returned to you (no cash for the contributed half, plus a betterment charge).

The arithmetic is uncomfortable but simple: **halve the area, double the effective rate.** A
₹3,000/sq yd Original Plot that yields a 50% Final Plot is really **₹6,000/sq yd of land you
can build on** — and that is before you confirm the final plot schedule is gazetted.

It is also the reason "Dholera land has multiplied fifty times" headlines are not what they
seem: they set the price of a *raw agricultural bigha* against the price of a *pooled,
NA-converted, serviced square yard*. Those are two different assets. The full deduction
mechanic, with worked numbers, is in the [plot price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## Statutory charges on top of the price

The asking rate is not your all-in cost. Expect roughly **4.9% stamp duty** (on the higher of
jantri or agreement value) plus **1% registration**, waived where a woman is the sole owner;
an **NA premium of 10% of jantri** (cut from 30% in November 2024); and note that **jantri
doubled statewide in April 2023**, so any valuation referencing the older figure is too low.
Current figures should be checked on the GARVI portal.

## Verify the village, then verify the plot

A village band describes the neighbourhood, not the parcel — two plots in the same village can
carry different schemes, road widths, zoning and reconstitution status. The full eight-check
evaluation framework is in [how to evaluate plots for sale in Dholera](https://dholeramap.com/blog/plots-for-sale-in-dholera);
in short, confirm the scheme and abutting road on the [TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map),
pull the [7/12 from AnyRoR](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records) yourself,
check the gazetted Form 4/5 final plot schedule, and search the survey number on the
[interactive cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) before the deed. Every village in the
table above links straight to its own indexed parcels.
