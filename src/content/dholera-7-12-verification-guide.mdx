A **7/12 — Satbara Utara** — is Gujarat's statutory village land record. In Dholera SIR it
is the single most important document in a land transaction, because it is the record the
revenue department holds, not a broker's claim.

Everything else in a Dholera deal — the price, the brochure, the agent's assurance — can be
re-written. The 7/12 cannot, which is exactly why it is the document you must obtain yourself.

## What "7/12" actually means

The name refers to two Gujarat village land-record forms combined into one extract:

- **Form 7** — the *record of rights*: who owns or holds rights over the parcel, and the
  chain of transfers (mutation entries).
- **Form 12** — the *agricultural land details*: the survey number, the area, the
  cultivation/land-use classification, and any encumbrances or charges.

Together they are the "7/12" or **Satbara Utara**, issued under the Gujarat land revenue
code. It is a **revenue record**, not a title guarantee — a clean 7/12 does not conclusively
prove that no one else has a claim, but a problematic 7/12 almost always means the deal is
dead.

> **The sibling record.** For urban/NA-converted property, the corresponding document is the
> **property card (Form 8-A / Card No. 6)**. Once a Dholera parcel is reconstituted into a
> serviced Final Plot and NA-converted, the relevant record can shift from the 7/12 toward
> the urban property card — so confirm which record the seller is actually showing you.

## What the 7/12 contains

| Field | What it means | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Survey number** | The revenue parcel ID | Must match the map exactly |
| **Area** | Plot area in sq m / hectares | Compare against what you are paying for |
| **Khatavahi / ownership** | Registered owner chain | Must chain to your seller |
| **Khata / mutation entries** | Title transfers (Fasal) | Gaps = unresolved ownership |
| **Cultivation / use** | Current recorded use | Non-agricultural use may be unauthorised |
| **Encumbrance notes** | Charges, court cases, mortgages | Any entry here is a hard stop |

## How to pull a 7/12 from AnyRoR yourself

**Never accept a photocopy, a photograph or a forwarded PDF.** Gujarat publishes these
records online through **AnyRoR** (Anywhere Record of Rights), the state's official land
records portal. The general workflow:

1. Open the Gujarat AnyRoR land-records portal and choose the **rural land record** (7/12 and
   8-A) service.
2. Select **Ahmedabad district → Dholera taluka → the relevant revenue village**.
3. Enter the **survey number** (or the survey/sub-survey the seller gives you).
4. Retrieve the extract and **save the dated copy with its reference details**.

If a seller will not give you the survey number so you can do this, that is the deal over —
there is no legitimate reason to withhold it.

> **Do it in your own name's session.** The point of pulling it yourself is that an unaltered,
> dated extract from the portal is evidence; a copy handed to you by the seller is not.

## The 4-step verification workflow

### 1. Pull the record yourself
As above — direct from AnyRoR, using the survey number and village. Cross-check the **area**
against what you are being charged for.

### 2. Match it to the cadastral map
Open the [DholeraMap atlas](https://dholeramap.com), search the survey number, and confirm
the parcel's shape, area, and boundaries match the 7/12. Discrepancies mean either the record
or the map is stale — and inside a TP scheme, boundaries change during reconstitution.

### 3. Check the final plot status
This is the step most buyers skip. In a sanctioned TP scheme, your agricultural survey
number is being reconstituted into a **Final Plot (FP)**. Confirm whether the
**Form 4 / Form 5** schedule has been gazetted, and what the FP number and area are. You
can cross-reference survey and final plots on the
[Dholera land records page](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records).

### 4. Verify the title chain and encumbrances
Read the mutation entries end-to-end. Look for:
- **Multiple unregistered transfers** — common in speculative Dholera deals.
- **Court cases or charges** — any pending litigation is a hard stop.
- **Co-ownership without all signatures** — every co-owner must consent.
- **Encumbrance or mortgage notes** — an entry here must be released before purchase.

## Dholera-specific traps

Dholera is not an ordinary Gujarat village transaction, and three local traps catch
first-time buyers:

- **The survey number may have changed.** During TP reconstitution, Original Plot survey
  numbers are replaced by Final Plot numbers under the gazetted Form 4/Form 5 schedule. A
  seller quoting an old survey number is not necessarily dishonest — the record may simply
  predate reconstitution — but **you must reconcile the old survey number to the current FP**,
  not assume they describe the same asset.
- **The land use may still read agricultural.** A plot sold as "residential" or "industrial"
  may still show agricultural use on the 7/12, meaning **NA conversion has not happened**.
  This is critical for NRIs — under FEMA, agricultural land is generally **not purchasable**
  by an NRI. See the [NRI investment guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/nri-investment-guide-dholera).
- **The 7/12 proves the land, not the building rights.** What you may build is set by the
  **TP scheme and abutting road width** under DGDCR (FAR 1.20–4.00, height 15–70 m), not by
  the revenue record. Check the [TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map).

## Red flags that should end the deal

- The survey number on the 7/12 does not exist on the cadastral map.
- The seller offers a "power of attorney" instead of a registered transfer deed.
- The plot is shown as agricultural but is being sold at industrial rates without NA
  permission.
- Area in the deed is "approximate."
- The mutation chain has gaps the seller cannot explain.
- The 7/12 is undated, unsealed, or produced only as a photocopy.

## The 7/12 is the floor, not the ceiling

A clean 7/12 proves title and area. It does **not** prove what you can build or what the
parcel is worth. For those you need the [TP scheme schedule and DGDCR road-width rules](https://dholeramap.com/guide)
— and a realistic check on [current plot prices](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-plot-price)
or the [village-wise land price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-village-wise-land-prices)
so you do not overpay for a plot whose building rights are limited.

For the full purchase evaluation, run the eight-check framework in
[how to evaluate plots for sale in Dholera](https://dholeramap.com/blog/plots-for-sale-in-dholera).

Verify everything on the map first; it costs nothing and catches most problems before they
reach a deed.
