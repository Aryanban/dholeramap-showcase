Search for "plots for sale in Dholera" and you will be flooded with price lists, "limited
inventory" urgency and WhatsApp numbers. Almost none of it answers the only question that
protects your money: **what exactly am I being sold, and what is it actually worth?**

DholeraMap does not sell plots and does not list inventory — we are an independent geospatial
compiler of government records. What we can give you is the **evaluation framework** a
professional buyer runs before writing a cheque. Use it on every parcel you are shown.

> **The core insight.** In Dholera, two plots in the same village, at the same asking price,
> can be entirely different assets — different TP schemes, different road widths, different
> development rights, different reconstitution status. The village name and the price tell you
> almost nothing. These eight checks tell you everything.

## Check 1: Is it inside the SIR boundary at all?

Land just outside the Dholera SIR boundary is routinely marketed as "in Dholera" but receives
**none** of the SIR benefits: no trunk infrastructure, no streamlined NA permission, no
reconstitution into a serviced Final Plot. Confirm the parcel falls inside the 920 sq km
statutory region on the [interactive cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) before
discussing price.

## Check 2: Which TP scheme is it in?

The region is planned through **six Town Planning schemes (TP 1–TP 6)**, and the scheme your
plot sits in determines its zoning, road frontage and building envelope. Inspect the scheme
boundaries on the [Dholera TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map).

Critically, **servicing status varies by scheme**:

- **TP 1 / TP 2 Activation Area** — live trunk infrastructure (underground power, recycled
  water, optical fibre, effluent, SCADA). Serviced industrial land here asks up to
  ~₹21,000/sq yd; serviced residential ₹11,000–16,000.
- **TP 3 – TP 6** — gazetted and planned, trunk infrastructure still rolling out. Asking
  ₹8,000–12,000, pricing in future servicing.

Paying a serviced price for an unserviced parcel is the single most common way buyers lose
money in Dholera.

## Check 3: What road does it abut?

This check is routinely skipped and it is worth real money. Under **DGDCR**, your permissible
**Floor Area Ratio is 1.20–4.00** and your maximum building height is **15–70 m**, determined
by the **width of the road your plot abuts**.

So an identical plot on a **55 m TP road** is worth materially more than one on an 18 m road —
because you may be allowed to build far more on it. Ask for the abutting road width *in
writing*, and verify it against the TP map, not the brochure.

## Check 4: Is reconstitution gazetted? (Form 4 / Form 5)

Most raw Dholera land is still **Original Plot** agricultural survey land. Through the SIR's
land-pooling mechanic, roughly **half the Original Plot's area is contributed** to public
infrastructure and a smaller, serviced **Final Plot** is returned — with no cash for the
contributed half and a betterment charge on top.

Ask to see the parcel's **Form 4 / Form 5 final plot schedule**. If it is not gazetted, you
are buying a promise, and the arithmetic turns against you:

> **Halve the area, double the rate.** A parcel quoted at ₹3,000/sq yd that yields a 50%
> Final Plot is really **₹6,000/sq yd** of land you can build on — before you even confirm the
> final plot exists.

This is why "Dholera land multiplied fifty times" headlines mislead: they set a **raw
agricultural bigha** against a **pooled, NA-converted, serviced square yard**. The worked
numbers are in the [plot price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## Check 5: Does the 7/12 say what you are told?

The **7/12 (Satbara Utara)** is the statutory revenue record of the parcel — its survey
number, area and land use. Never accept a photocopy, a photograph or a broker's summary.

1. Pull it **directly from AnyRoR** yourself. Follow the
   [7/12 verification workflow](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records).
2. Confirm the **survey number matches** the parcel on the
   [cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) — boundaries and FP numbers change during
   reconstitution, so an old map can mislead.
3. Confirm the **land use**. If it still reads agricultural and you are being sold a
   "residential plot", the NA conversion has not happened yet. For NRIs this is not a detail —
   under FEMA, agricultural land is generally **not purchasable** by an NRI. Read the
   [NRI investment guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/nri-investment-guide-dholera).
4. Confirm the **area** — and remember the ~50% deduction when comparing an OP area against
   the FP you will actually receive.

## Check 6: Is the price honest?

Dholera has **no official per-plot or per-village transaction price**. The one published
figure is the **DICDL allotment card** — a 99-year leasehold priced per square metre by
land-use zone (₹4,000–8,000, solar ₹400), last updated 24 August 2026. It is a **floor**, not
a market rate, and it excludes GST, registration, stamp duty and land filling.

So benchmark any quote a seller gives you against three things: the
[DICDL card and market-rate analysis](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026),
the [village-wise indicative bands](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-village-wise-land-prices),
and — most importantly — whether the parcel actually has the **servicing** (Check 2) and the
**road frontage** (Check 3) that the price is asking you to pay for.

If the quote assumes infrastructure or a road width that is not there, the price is not honest
for that parcel.

## Check 7: Have you counted the statutory charges?

Buyers budget the price and forget the rest. On top of it expect roughly **4.9% stamp duty**
(on the higher of jantri or agreement value), **1% registration** (waived for a woman as sole
owner), and the **NA premium at 10% of jantri** — cut from 30% in November 2024. GST, land
filling and development charges are case-specific, so get those in writing.

One trap worth knowing: **jantri doubled statewide in April 2023**, so any figure quoting the
older valuation materially understates your real cost. Check current jantri on the GARVI
portal.

## Check 8: Who is selling, and do they survive verification?

- If a **broker or agent** is involved, ask whether they are a **registered Gujarat RERA
  agent** and ask for their registration number. Read our guide to choosing
  [real estate agents in Dholera](https://dholeramap.com/blog/real-estate-agents-in-dholera).
- If the seller is **not the revenue-record owner**, demand the documented authority chain.
- Never pay a "token" to "hold" a parcel before the 7/12 and Form 4/5 checks pass. Genuine
  sellers wait for due diligence.
- Get every material fact — TP scheme, road width, FP number, NA status, area — **in the
  agreement**, not in a WhatsApp message.

## The evaluation checklist

Run this on every Dholera parcel you are shown:

- [ ] Inside the SIR boundary (cadastral atlas)
- [ ] TP scheme confirmed (TP map) and servicing status known
- [ ] Abutting road width confirmed in writing (sets FAR 1.20–4.00, height 15–70 m)
- [ ] Form 4/5 final plot schedule obtained; is reconstitution gazetted?
- [ ] 7/12 pulled from AnyRoR by you; survey number, area and land use match
- [ ] Land use is NA residential/commercial (critical for NRI buyers under FEMA)
- [ ] Price benchmarked against DICDL card and village bands, adjusted for servicing
- [ ] Stamp duty, registration, NA premium and jantri position all counted
- [ ] Seller identity and RERA registration verified
- [ ] Every material fact written into the agreement

If a seller cannot pass these checks, the correct response is to walk away — there are plenty
of parcels in Dholera that can.
