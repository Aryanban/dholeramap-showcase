The price of a Dholera SIR plot is only half the equation. The other half is **what the law
lets you build on it** — and that is decided by a single document: the **Development Control
and Building Regulations (DGDCR)** for the region.

Two plots of identical size, a few hundred metres apart, can have wildly different value
because one permits a four-storey building and the other a fifteen-storey one. This guide
explains how the rules work, using the same statutory framework behind the
[DholeraMap cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com).

## DGDCR in one sentence

**Your building rights are set by the width of the road your plot abuts.**

That is the entire system. Wider road = more permissible Floor Area Ratio (FAR) = more
built-up area = more value per square yard. The DGDCR ties vertical growth to the public
right-of-way so that dense buildings only rise where the infrastructure can carry them.

## What FAR / FSI actually means

FAR (Floor Area Ratio) and FSI (Floor Space Index) are the same number expressed differently.
Both compare the **total built-up floor area** of all floors with the **land area** of the
plot:

> FAR = Total built-up area of every floor ÷ Plot land area

A FAR of **2.00** on a 100 sq yard (900 sq ft) plot lets you construct **1,800 sq ft** of
built-up floor space — spread across as many floors as the height limit allows.

## The Dholera FAR matrix

Under the DGDCR, permissible FAR and maximum building height step up with the abutting road
width:

| Abutting road width | Max building height | Indicative FAR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18m | ~15 m | 1.20 – 1.80 |
| 30m | ~30 m | 2.00 – 2.50 |
| 55m | ~50 m | 3.00+ |
| 70m+ | up to 70 m | up to 4.00 |

The exact figure within each band depends on the zoning of the block — residential R-1,
commercial, or industrial — so two plots on the same road can still differ. Treat the table
as the statutory range, not a quote for your specific plot.

## Worked example: the same land, two roads

Take a **100 sq yard** plot and apply the DGDCR bands:

- **On an 18m road** (FAR ~1.50): roughly **1,350 sq ft** of buildable floor area, within a
  ~15 m height limit — typically a G+4 format building.
- **On a 55m road** (FAR ~3.00): roughly **2,700 sq ft** of buildable floor area, within a
  ~50 m height limit — a mid-rise tower.

The land area is identical. The buildable area **doubles**, purely because of the road the
plot faces. This is why the [TP schemes guide](https://dholeramap.com/guide) treats road
frontage, not plot size, as the primary driver of land value in Dholera.

## Buying extra FAR

FAR beyond the base allowance is not a hard ceiling. Additional FSI can be procured by paying
**Betterment / FSI Purchase charges** directly to the development authority. If you are
underwriting a project, price the base FAR conservatively and treat purchasable FSI as
optional upside — never bank on it in the land cost.

## Why this matters before you buy, not after

The DGDCR rewards buyers who check the road first. Before committing to a plot:

1. **Confirm the TP scheme.** The six Town Planning schemes set the road network and zoning;
   see the [TP 1–6 schemes map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map).
2. **Verify the final plot, not the old survey parcel.** Reconstitution changes boundaries and
   frontage. Cross-check the gazetted Final Plot schedule against the
   [7/12 (Satbara) record](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records).
3. **Read the abutting road width off the map.** That single number tells you the FAR band
   your project will live in.
