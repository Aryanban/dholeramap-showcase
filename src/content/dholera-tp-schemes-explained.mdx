Dholera's six Town Planning schemes are the legal skeleton of the city. If you own or are
buying land here, the **TP scheme number** determines your plot's value more than any other
single factor — it sets your road access, your zoning, and therefore how much you can
legally build. This is the statutory framework behind the interactive
[DholeraMap atlas](https://dholeramap.com), where every scheme boundary is georeferenced
and searchable by survey number.

## What a TP scheme actually does

Under the **Gujarat Town Planning and Urban Development (GTPUD) Act 1976**, a Town Planning
scheme reconstitutes scattered agricultural holdings into serviced, legally-titled building
plots. It is the mechanism that converts farmland into a city, and it works in three steps:

1. **Original Plot (OP)** — your existing agricultural survey parcel, as recorded in the
   revenue 7/12 (Satbara). Read our
   [7/12 verification guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-7-12-verification-guide)
   to confirm yours is clean before anything else.
2. **Deduction** — roughly **50%** of gross area goes to roads, parks, stormwater drains,
   utility corridors and social infrastructure. This is the statutory land contribution that
   funds the city's infrastructure; it is not a tax, and it is why a Final Plot costs more
   per square yard than the agricultural land it came from.
3. **Final Plot (FP)** — the serviced, reconstituted plot you receive, with legal road
   frontage, non-agricultural (NA) status, and a Gazette Form 4 / Form 5 schedule that is
   the document of title.

The deduction is the single biggest source of buyer confusion, and it is why asking prices
per square yard vary so wildly. We break the arithmetic down in the
[2026 plot price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## The six schemes at a glance

| Scheme | Key villages | Road widths | Dominant zoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TP 1 | Ambli, Bhadiyad, Bhimtalav, Gogla, Kadipur, Khun, Umargadh | 18m / 30m / 55m / 70m | Residential R-1, commercial |
| TP 2 (2A & 2B) | Hebatpur, Bhimnath, Gorasu | 18m / 30m / 55m / 250m | Industrial, semiconductor |
| TP 3 | Dholera town, Sandhida, Sangasar | 18m / 30m / 55m | Commercial core, mixed-use |
| TP 4 | Mundi | 18m / 30m / 55m / 70m | Renewable, solar logistics |
| TP 5 | Bavaliyari | 18m / 30m / 55m | Aviation MRO, cargo |
| TP 6 | Bhangadh, Zankhi, southern Bavaliyari | 30m / 55m / 70m | Heavy industry, maritime |

> Village assignments follow the published cadastre in the DholeraMap registry,
> which records the actual scheme sheet every parcel falls on — not the master
> plan's indicative boundaries. Search any village on the

### TP 1 — The Activation Area
Covers **Ambli, Bhadiyad, Bhimtalav, Gogla, Kadipur, Khun and Umargadh**. The immediate 22.5 sq km Activation Area sits
here, and it is the only part of the SIR with completed trunk infrastructure: underground
power, treated and recycled water, SCADA monitoring, and optic fibre to the plot boundary.
Roads of 18m / 30m / 55m / 70m. Dominant zoning is Residential R-1, high-tech commercial,
and city-centre administrative. Because utilities are actually in the ground, serviced
plots here trade at a premium — see
[what is built in the Activation Area](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-activation-area-utilities).

### TP 2 (2A & 2B) — High-access industrial core
Covers **Hebatpur**, with **Bhimnath** in the rail-terminal sector and **Gorasu** in the
heavy-industry belt. It
includes the 250m expressway corridor and the semiconductor fab belt anchored by the
Tata-PSMC plant — the largest single private investment in Indian manufacturing history,
which we track in
[Tata Semiconductor fab status](https://dholeramap.com/blog/tata-semiconductor-dholera-fab-status).
This is where heavy manufacturing, logistics and the fab supply chain concentrate, and it
is the scheme that drives industrial land demand across the whole SIR.

### TP 3 — Commercial and financial core
Covers **Dholera town, Sandhida and Sangasar**. The commercial core holds institutional finance,
mixed-use urban blocks and city-centre retail on 18m / 30m / 55m roads. If you are buying
for commercial use rather than industrial, this is the scheme with the highest allowable
Floor Area Ratio on wide roads.

### TP 4 — Renewable energy belt
Covers **Mundi**. Solar park logistics, 5,000 MW renewable capacity and
green hydrogen — the clean-tech quadrant. Land here is priced for industrial use but zoned
for the renewable supply chain, which makes it the most volatile scheme by price.

### TP 5 — Aerotropolis
Covers **Bavaliyari**, the expressway-side logistics and aviation-support village. Aviation MRO, air cargo and
warehousing that backs the greenfield Dholera International Airport at Navagam. Its value
thesis depends on the airport and the
[expressway](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-expressway-airport-operational), both of
which are now operational rather than promised.

### TP 6 — Coastal heavy industry
Covers **Bhangadh, Zankhi and southern Bavaliyari**. Heavy engineering and maritime supply chain, with
direct access to the Gulf of Khambhat coastline. This is the largest scheme by area and the
slowest to develop; treat any near-term appreciation claim here with scepticism.

## DGDCR: what you can actually build

The **Development Control and Building Regulations (DGDCR)** tie building rights to the road
your plot abuts. This is the rule that makes two identically-sized plots worth very
different amounts:

| Road width | Max height | Indicative FAR | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 18m | ~15 m | 1.20 – 1.80 | Row houses, low-rise apartments |
| 30m | ~30 m | 2.00 – 2.50 | Mid-rise residential, commercial |
| 55m | ~50 m | 3.00+ | High-rise, high-access commercial |
| 70m+ | up to 70 m | up to 4.00 | Iconic towers, city-centre mixed-use |

> A plot on a 55m road is not "bigger" — it is **worth more per square yard** because the
> law lets you build substantially more on it.

For the worked examples of how FAR translates into buildable floor area, read

The full statutory table is reproduced in the
[DholeraMap cadastral guide](https://dholeramap.com/guide).

## How to check which scheme your plot is in

Three steps, in order:

1. Look up the survey number on the
   [DholeraMap TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map) — the interactive atlas covers
   all six schemes and resolves to individual Final Plots.
2. Confirm the final plot schedule against the
   [7/12 record](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records). Reconstitution
   changes boundaries — always verify against the gazetted schedule, not the old survey map.
3. Cross-check pricing for that scheme in the
   [2026 plot price rate card](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-plot-price) before you negotiate.

If you are evaluating a plot as an investment rather than a place to build, the scheme
number also tells you which risks apply. See
[Is Dholera SIR a good investment in 2026?](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-sir-investment-2026)
for the risk framework by scheme and zone.

For a plain-language definition of what "SIR" status itself means legally, start with
[Dholera SIR full form](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-sir-full-form).
