Dholera SIR is marketed hard to NRIs — usually with a price chart and a promise. What is
far less often explained is that buying land in India from abroad is governed by **FEMA**,
and FEMA draws a hard line between two kinds of Dholera land. Getting that distinction wrong
is the single most common way an NRI loses money here.

This guide covers the rules that actually apply in 2026, the remittance mechanics, the tax
on resale, and the due-diligence chain you can run remotely.

> **Important.** FEMA and income-tax provisions are amended frequently. This guide explains
> the framework and the questions to ask; it is not tax advice. Before remitting funds or
> signing an agreement, confirm the current position with a **FEMA-qualified chartered
> accountant** and, where required, your bank's authorised dealer.

## The FEMA line: agricultural land vs a serviced Final Plot

Under FEMA, an NRI or OCI may generally purchase **residential and commercial property** in
India. What you may **not** generally purchase is **agricultural land, plantation property
or a farmhouse**.

This matters enormously in Dholera because much of the SIR starts as **revenue
(agricultural) survey land**. So there are two very different assets:

- **Raw agricultural survey land** in a Dholera village — generally **not** purchasable by
  an NRI under the general permission, regardless of what a seller tells you.
- **A reconstituted, NA-converted Final Plot (FP)** inside a sanctioned Town Planning
  scheme, with residential or commercial zoning and the land-use changed on the revenue
  record — a fundamentally different asset that is generally within the permission.

> **The question to ask before anything else:** *"Is this parcel a gazetted Final Plot with
> NA sanction and residential/commercial zoning, or is it still agricultural survey land?"*
> If the answer is vague, stop. Use the
> [DholeraMap cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) to confirm the survey number and its
> TP scheme, then verify the land use on the [7/12 record](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records).

## How Dholera land becomes NRI-eligible: the reconstitution chain

Understanding the mechanic also explains the price. Under the **GTPUD Act 1976** and the
**SIR Act 2009**, Dholera uses **land pooling**, not cash acquisition:

1. Agricultural survey parcels (Original Plots, OP) are pooled into a Town Planning scheme.
2. Roughly **50% of the gross area is deducted** for roads, utilities and parks.
3. Landowners receive back a smaller, serviced **Final Plot (FP)** with legal road frontage.
4. The plot receives non-agricultural (NA) sanction and a clear land-use classification.

This is why an OP quoted cheaply per square yard is not cheap: a 50% FP doubles your
effective per-yard cost of deliverable land. **Price the Final Plot, not the Original Plot.**
It is also why "raw land multiplied 50×" claims mislead — they compare two different assets.

## Remitting the funds: the LRS limit

To fund an Indian property purchase, an NRI remits under the **Liberalised Remittance Scheme
(LRS)**. The key constraint:

- **US$ 250,000 per financial year, per person.**
- The remittance must go to your own **NRO/NRE account**; from there it is disbursed to the
  seller.
- Your **PAN** is mandatory, and the purpose code must correctly declare property purchase.
- Married couples sometimes structure as two remitters — but the ownership must then
  genuinely reflect two buyers on the deed. Do not split a single purchase artificially.

If the purchase exceeds the annual cap, plan the remittance across financial years and agree
a staged payment schedule with the seller — before you commit, not after.

> **Never pay a seller directly from abroad** in the hope of sorting paperwork later. The
> authorised dealer route is what makes the money legitimately repatriable later.

## Tax when you sell: TDS catches NRIs differently

The biggest surprise for NRI sellers is **how TDS works**. On the sale of Indian property:

- A **resident** seller faces 1% TDS, and only above a threshold.
- An **NRI** seller faces **TDS on the capital gain itself — 20% on long-term capital gains,
  plus applicable surcharge and cess — regardless of the sale value.** The buyer is legally
  obliged to deduct it.

Because Dholera plots are typically held for more than 24 months, gains are long-term. Note
that from July 2024 the long-term capital-gains computation on real estate changed (a lower
rate without indexation, with a beneficial-option comparison) — **confirm which computation
applies to your specific sale** with a CA before you agree a price, because the deduction is
taken at source and recovering an over-deduction means filing for a refund.

Other points that bite:

- **The 7/12 and the deed must match.** If the consideration is split between "plot value"
  and "development charges" to lower the apparent price, the 7/12 and the TDS computation
  still reference the full consideration. Under-reporting creates a FEMA and tax exposure.
- **Capital-gains reinvestment** sections can reduce the taxable gain if you reinvest into
  specified residential property or bonds — plan this before the sale, not after.

## Repatriating the sale proceeds

After the sale and TDS, repatriation is allowed for **up to two residential properties**
per NRI under the general permission, subject to the **US$ 1 million per financial year**
repatriation cap (composite ceiling across all current-account remittances), with the
original investment and capital gains properly documented through an authorised dealer.

The practical requirement is a clean **document chain**: the FEMA-compliant remittance
trail, the PAN, the TDS certificate (Form 16A / 27AS), and the registered deed. Break the
chain at the purchase stage and repatriation becomes a problem years later.

## Running due diligence from abroad

You can do most of this remotely, and it is exactly what protects you:

1. **Verify the parcel** — search the survey number on the
   [interactive cadastral atlas](https://dholeramap.com) and confirm its TP scheme, zone and
   whether reconstitution is gazetted. Check the [TP map](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-tp-map)
   for the abutting road width, which sets your DGDCR building envelope (FAR 1.20–4.00).
2. **Pull the 7/12 from AnyRoR yourself** — never accept a photocopy or a broker's
   screenshot. Follow the [7/12 verification workflow](https://dholeramap.com/dholera-7-12-anyror-land-records).
3. **Confirm the land use is NA** and that the classification is residential or commercial —
   not agricultural. This is the FEMA gate.
4. **Confirm the Form 4/5 final plot schedule** — the actual FP number and area, post-deduction.
5. **Check price honesty** against the [2026 village-wise land price guide](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-village-wise-land-prices)
   and the [DICDL rate card](https://dholeramap.com/blog/dholera-plot-price-september-2026).

## Power of Attorney: the biggest remote risk

Most NRIs transact through a **Power of Attorney (POA)** held by a relative or associate in
India. This is normal, but it is where the largest fraud exposures occur:

- Grant a **specific POA** for one identified property, not a general POA over your affairs.
- Insist the POA be **registered**, not merely notarised, and that it recites the exact
  survey number and FP number.
- Prefer an **NRI-specific bank mandate** so funds release only against registered documents.
- If the POA holder is also the broker or seller's representative, **that is a conflict of
  interest** — refuse the arrangement.

## The honest investment case

Dholera SIR is **partially operational**: the TP 1 / TP 2 Activation Area has live trunk
infrastructure, the Tata semiconductor fab is under construction, and the 109 km expressway
is functional. TP 3–TP 6 are planned and gazetted with infrastructure still rolling out. So
an NRI investment here is a **long-hold, infrastructure-timing play**, not a short-term flip.

Price the serviced Final Plot, respect the FEMA gate, keep the document chain clean, and
treat any "guaranteed quick doubling" pitch as a reason to walk away.
